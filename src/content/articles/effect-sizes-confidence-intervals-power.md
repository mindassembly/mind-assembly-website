## Magnitude and precision

An effect size expresses the magnitude of a result. It can be a difference in the original units, a standardised difference or a measure of association. Choose the units that help readers judge the practical meaning.

Suppose a study estimates a 3-point improvement on a questionnaire, with a 95% confidence interval from −1 to 7 points. If 5 points is the smallest worthwhile improvement, this interval is compatible with a worthwhile benefit and with little benefit or slight worsening, given the method's assumptions. Under repeated use of a valid 95% interval procedure, about 95% of the intervals would cover the true parameter.

## What was the study designed to detect?

Statistical power is the probability that a specified test rejects the null under a specified alternative and its design assumptions. It depends on factors such as sample size, variability, significance threshold and the effect being considered. A study can have high power for a large effect and low power for a small but meaningful one.

Check whether the authors justified the target effect and sample size in advance. For an inconclusive result, read the confidence interval to see which effects remain compatible with the data. A wide interval can leave important possibilities open.
