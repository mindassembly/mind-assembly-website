## Three processes, not one storage box

Psychologists commonly distinguish **encoding**, **storage**, and **retrieval**. What is noticed and meaningfully processed at encoding shapes what can later be recalled. Retrieval is also affected by the cues available at the time of remembering.

## Why confidence and accuracy may diverge

A memory can feel clear without preserving every detail accurately. People may mix information from related events, rely on expectations, or confuse where they first encountered a piece of information. These possibilities are especially important when interpreting eyewitness accounts or highly emotional autobiographical memories.

This does **not** mean memory is useless or that every recollection is false. It means memory is a psychological process rather than a perfect video archive.

## A worked example

A person remembers the atmosphere of a crowded classroom vividly but misremembers where one particular student was sitting. Their emotional impression can be genuine even if the spatial detail is mistaken.

## What to read critically

When evaluating research, ask how memory was measured: free recall, recognition, confidence ratings, or a structured interview? These tasks ask different questions, and their results should not be treated as interchangeable.

## Reading note

For further study, examine experimental designs that separate what participants experienced from what they later report.
