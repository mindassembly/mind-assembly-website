## What was originally studied

Attachment research examines patterns in the ways children seek proximity and security with caregivers. Ainsworth's *Strange Situation* procedure classified patterns of infant behaviour during a structured sequence of separations and reunions.

The familiar categories—often called secure, avoidant, and resistant/ambivalent, with disorganised patterns described subsequently—come from observational research traditions. They are not labels for a person's moral character.

## Why context matters

Attachment-related behaviour depends on a relationship, a developmental period, an assessment procedure, and a particular context. An infant classification cannot simply be read as a lifelong prediction for every relationship.

## What gets oversimplified online

Popular discussions often treat 'attachment styles' as fixed personality types. That framing can obscure variability, the difference between infant attachment research and adult self-report measures, and the role of changing relationships and environments.

## A better question

Instead of asking whether someone *is* a category, ask what particular behaviour was observed, using which measure, in which relationship, at what age, and with what alternative explanations.
