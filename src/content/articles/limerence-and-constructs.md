## An interesting word is not a settled construct

**Limerence** is used in psychology-related literature to describe an intense, often intrusive form of romantic preoccupation. It has attracted more empirical attention, but the boundaries separating it from related experiences—such as infatuation, obsessive rumination, and attachment-related distress—remain important research questions.

## Where the literature has moved

A 2024 scoping review surveyed relevant literature and highlighted conceptual and clinical questions. Later measurement work introduced an 11-item Limerence Questionnaire (LQ-11) with initial validation evidence. A 2026 investigation examined features and correlates in larger samples. These developments matter, but they do not make the phenomenon a formal psychiatric diagnosis.

## Three questions researchers should separate

- **Definition:** What experiences must be present to justify the label?
- **Measurement:** Which aspects are captured by questionnaire items, and in which samples were they tested?
- **Explanation:** What factors predict onset, course, distress, or impairment?

A measure's existence does not resolve all three questions. Construct validity and cultural transfer require their own evidence.

## Editorial caution

Avoid telling someone they 'have limerence' based on a brief description or a score. This is a developing research area, not a substitute for assessment or support when someone is distressed.
