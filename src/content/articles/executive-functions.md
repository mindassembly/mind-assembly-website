## A family of related abilities

**Executive functions** are processes involved in guiding behaviour toward goals, especially when habits or distractions are not enough. A widely used account identifies three core processes: working memory, inhibitory control, and cognitive flexibility.

- **Working memory** supports holding and manipulating information over short intervals.
- **Inhibitory control** supports resisting an automatic response or interference.
- **Cognitive flexibility** helps someone shift between rules, tasks, or ways of thinking.

These functions interact. They are not three completely separate compartments of the brain.

## Why everyday descriptions can mislead

Calling someone 'poor at self-control' compresses many possibilities into a single judgement. A person may struggle to retain instructions, shift tasks, filter competing information, or regulate a habitual response. The most useful question is often *which demand* is difficult, and under *which conditions*.

## Measurement matters

Different neuropsychological tests capture different task demands. Performance can also reflect processing speed, language, motivation, anxiety, fatigue, and familiarity with the task. No single brief task is a complete measure of executive functioning.

## A useful distinction

The construct is relevant to everyday difficulties, but a poor score or an executive-function complaint does not by itself establish a clinical diagnosis. Interpretation depends on a wider assessment.
