## Consistency does not establish meaning

A questionnaire may produce scores that are internally consistent without adequately representing the construct researchers intend to measure. **Reliability** concerns consistency and sources of measurement error; **validity** concerns the evidence supporting how scores are interpreted and used.

## Three practical checks

1. **Content:** Do the items cover the intended construct, and are they comprehensible to the target respondents?
2. **Structure:** Do the observed item patterns fit the claimed dimension or dimensions?
3. **Context:** Has the measure been examined in the population, language, and setting you plan to study?

COSMIN guidance places strong emphasis on content validity when evaluating patient-reported measures. This provides a useful general lesson, although particular standards depend on the purpose of a scale.

## Why an established scale may still be unsuitable

Imagine a measure developed among adults in one language being used with adolescents in a different context. Even a high coefficient reported in the original paper cannot automatically show that the items are suitable, understandable, or culturally equivalent in the new group.

## A study-planning habit

Before downloading any questionnaire, write down the construct definition, target population, scoring interpretation, development study, and what validity evidence exists *for that use*. A familiar measure is not automatically the right measure.
