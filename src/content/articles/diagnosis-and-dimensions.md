## Categories answer particular questions

Diagnostic systems can support clinical communication, service decisions, research inclusion and treatment planning. But two individuals meeting criteria for the same diagnosis may differ substantially in symptoms, history, co-occurring problems and everyday functioning.

## The dimensional alternative

The NIMH **Research Domain Criteria (RDoC)** initiative encourages study of processes across ranges of functioning, using multiple levels of analysis. It is a research framework rather than a replacement diagnostic manual for clinical use.

## Not an either/or choice

Categorical and dimensional approaches serve different purposes. A diagnostic category might be useful for a clinical decision; dimensional assessment may better capture severity, variation, or change in a particular process. Neither alone provides a complete description of a person's experience.

## Reading critically

When a paper links a diagnosis to a brain measure, ask whether the reported effect is specific to that diagnosis, overlaps across groups, or varies within groups. Avoid assuming that a statistical group difference can classify an individual with certainty.

## Takeaway

A good scientific explanation specifies what its categories help us do—and what remains outside those categories.
