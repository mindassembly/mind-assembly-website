## Norms are social information

A **social norm** is a shared expectation about behaviour within a particular group or setting. Some norms are explicit rules; others are inferred from the reactions of people around us.

Social psychology distinguishes pressures to fit in or be accepted from reliance on a group for information about what is correct. The same outward behaviour can arise for different reasons.

## The context problem

Silence in a classroom may reflect respect, fear of embarrassment, uncertainty, or a norm about when to speak. Observing silence alone does not tell us which explanation applies.

## Why this matters in psychological interpretation

Labelling a behaviour unusual or maladaptive without examining its environment risks mistaking a contextual adaptation for a personal deficit. Yet context does not make every behaviour harmless; effects on wellbeing and functioning still matter.

## Questions for researchers

What group defines the norm? Is the behaviour measured privately or publicly? How does the norm differ across settings, ages and cultural contexts? Answering these questions helps avoid broad claims from narrow samples.
